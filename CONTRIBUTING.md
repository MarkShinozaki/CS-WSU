# Contributing

Thanks for helping improve this course hub and the linked repositories. Contributions make these materials more useful for everyone learning computer science at WSU and beyond.

## Academic integrity

Use these repositories to **learn**, understand concepts, and practice—not to submit someone else's work for graded assignments, exams, or projects. Follow [Washington State University](https://wsu.edu/) and your instructors' academic integrity policies. When in doubt, ask your professor or TA.

## What you can contribute

- Fixes to broken links, typos, or formatting in this hub (`README.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`)
- Clearer notes, study guides, or explanations in course repos
- Improved solutions or examples (where appropriate for learning)
- Additional resources that complement official coursework
- Corrections to organization or documentation

If a change belongs in a specific course repo (e.g. CPTS 122 labs), open the PR there when possible; otherwise start here and we can coordinate.

## How to contribute

1. **Fork** [MarkShinozaki/CS-WSU](https://github.com/MarkShinozaki/CS-WSU) on GitHub (or the relevant course repository).
2. **Clone** your fork locally.
3. **Create a branch** with a clear name, for example:
   - `fix/readme-typo`
   - `docs/add-study-notes`
4. **Make your changes** and commit with a short, descriptive message.
5. **Push** to your fork and open a **Pull Request** against the `main` branch.
6. In the PR description, explain what you changed and why.

## Pull request tips

- Keep changes focused; one logical improvement per PR when possible.
- Preserve existing links and facts unless you are fixing an verified error.
- Do not add copyrighted material (e.g. full textbook scans) without permission.
- Be respectful and constructive in review discussions.

Questions or unsure where to contribute? Open an issue on this repository.
